What does flick-dry mean?

flick-dry

verb · flick-dries; flick-dried; flick-drying

To flick excess water off, then dry.

noun

A drying routine that flicks off excess water before drying.

How to flick-dry hands

Flick first. Then dry completely.

Choose the version for your washroom. The flick is the same.

Hand dryer Paper towels

Safety first. Follow facility instructions. Avoid splashing people or nearby surfaces. Skip flicking if it is unsafe or difficult for you.

HAND DRYER

Flick-dry with a hand dryer

  1. Wash with soap and water, then rinse.
  2. Gently flick excess water off your hands into the sink.
  3. Use the hand dryer until your hands are completely dry.

PAPER TOWELS

Flick-dry with paper towels

  1. Wash with soap and water, then rinse.
  2. Gently flick excess water off your hands into the sink.
  3. Use one paper towel, folding it if that helps, and dry thoroughly. Take more if you need them.
  4. Put used paper towels in the bin.

Research

Literature Review to Community Pilot

So far, our research has included three stages. First, we reviewed published studies on flicking water off the hands before drying. Next, we measured how long electric hand dryers ran when using the flick-dry method compared with drying directly without flicking. In the third stage, we promoted the habit in local businesses. Feedback from volunteer outreach led us to add guidance for paper-towel use as well. We have not yet measured whether this guidance has reduced paper-towel use.

STAGE 1 · LITERATURE REVIEW

Flicking Water Off Before Hand Drying: A Literature Review

Abstract

Flicking water off washed hands before using a towel or dryer is a plausible way to save resources, but its effect must be separated from the drying that follows. Flicking already appears in experimental protocols. Patrick et al. used two flicks, and Suen et al. used at least five shakes before drying.[1][7] Environmental comparisons depend on equipment and modeling assumptions.[1][3][11] No source shows how flick count translates into water removed or towels or electricity saved, so this review supports dose-controlled evaluation of pre-flicking.

STAGE 2 · EXPLORATORY

Flick-dry vs. direct-dry: dryer runtime

Analysis snapshot · September 6, 2026

14 exports, 102 unique runs, and 15 provisional comparison sets. After six invalid measurements are excluded, 96 reviewed runs contribute to within-set comparisons.

Every comparison set recorded a lower median dryer runtime with flick-dry. Savings may vary across users and dryers. All runs used electric hand dryers.

STAGE 3 · COMMUNITY PILOT

What we learned from local community visits

Nine businesses currently display our educational sticker. Volunteer outreach also revealed an important gap: many locations rely on paper towels rather than electric hand dryers, which our original dryer-only sticker did not address. In response, we added paper-towel guidance, created a supporting video, and developed a separate infographic for paper-towel use so that the initiative now guides both hand-drying methods.

Our education-first app focuses on flick-dry for hand-drying, uses the microphone to estimate hand-dryer running time, and asks whether each use included flick-dry. Select the same physical dryer to compare uses involving flick-dry or direct-dry, with per-use and cumulative estimated time and energy changes.

Measurements stay local to your phone. No raw audio is saved, and no account or upload server is required. An optional ZIP export lets you choose whether to email your measurements. Monitoring has a manual Stop control and an automatic timeout.

The app currently supports hand dryers only. Using paper towels? Follow the paper-towel steps.

About the initiative

Student-led. Open to public education collaborations.

Visiting science museums in different cities has always been part of my family vacation. After visiting so many science museums, the exhibits started to blend. However, one I still recall vividly was an exhibit about flicking excess water off your hands to use less paper to dry them. What struck me was the idea that such a small, seemingly insignificant action could have a meaningful impact. The specific museum and city have long since slipped my mind, but the idea the exhibit presented stayed with me. It made me wonder how I could apply this concept to a society increasingly dependent on electricity; in particular, I wondered how flicking excess water could reduce the energy consumption of electric hand dryers.

That question sparked the Flick-dry Initiative. After reviewing current research, I developed app prototypes and collected and analyzed data. The early app prototypes didn’t work. Rather than keep adjusting them, I switched to a data-driven approach: I recorded dryers running, labeled the data, and used those labels to develop the Flick First Dryer Verifier app. This approach produced a working tool for recording dryer running times and supporting comparisons between flick-dry and direct-dry trials.

My goal is to bring this work into public education. Online, through this website, the Flick-dry app, email, and social media, I want to help people around the world understand and try a simple habit. Locally, our volunteers visit businesses, schools, and community organizations to share it in person. By collaborating with them, I hope to turn the vision into practice—small changes in daily habits that make a big impact on resource savings.

While placing Flick-dry stickers with local businesses, our volunteers noticed that many of the washrooms they visited use paper towels, not hand dryers. That brought the project full circle—the museum exhibit that started it all was about using less paper. So this website now shows how to flick-dry with either a hand dryer or paper towels.

Website analytics and privacy

Our homepage, research pages, and sticker page load a Cloudflare analytics script in your browser. It sends page-view and loading-performance measurements to Cloudflare so we can understand website reach and usability. Reports can include page paths, referring websites, country, and browser/device categories. Cloudflare states that Web Analytics does not track individuals across websites.

Cloudflare receives the network requests, including the IP address needed to deliver them; cookieless analytics does not mean no data is processed. Cloudflare currently describes access to the previous six months of analytics, with unsampled beacon data retained for seven days and reduced data used afterward. See Cloudflare’s analytics documentation and privacy policy for its current practices.

The hand-dryer and paper-towel videos on this page are embedded from YouTube in its privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). Your browser loads the players from YouTube when you scroll to them, which sends YouTube your IP address and browser details; playing a video is subject to Google’s privacy policy. You can also watch the hand-dryer video or the paper-towel video on YouTube directly.

This website analytics is separate from the Flick-dry app and research records. We do not add names, email addresses, recordings, or participant identifiers to analytics.
