Stage 1: Literature Review

Our literature review brings together hand-drying research, engineering descriptions, environmental assessments and the popular ‘shake and fold’ paper-towel demonstration.

MECHANISM ≠ EFFECT SIZE

How much water comes off?

Flicking removes excess surface water before drying, but the sources do not show how much water a given number of flicks removes.

PRIOR PROTOCOLS

Has flicking been tested on its own?

No. Patrick et al. used two flicks, and Suen et al. used at least five shakes before comparing drying methods [1][7]. These studies did not separate the effect of flicking itself from the drying that follows.

REAL-WORLD VALUE

Does it save useful resources?

Environmental comparisons of hand drying depend on equipment, resource use, and modeling assumptions.[1][3][11] The sources do not show how much flicking saves in towels or electricity at the same final dryness.

What the review contributes

The review identifies relevant prior work, connects water removal, dryer running time, and resource savings, and names the questions the sources leave open.
