STAGE 2 · EXPLORATORY ARCHIVE ANALYSIS · VERSION 1

Shorter recorded dryer runtimes.
Less energy used.

Analysis snapshot: September 6, 2026. Measurement period: August 18–September 5, 2026. This public edition summarizes an existing archive analysis, not a new controlled experiment or peer-reviewed paper.

Interpretation first: All 15 provisional comparison sets show lower median recorded runtime for flick-dry. This is descriptive evidence worth investigating—showing shorter dryer running times and the corresponding reduction in energy use. The practical research focus is reducing dryer running time to save energy.

Dataset and accounting

14 ZIP exports contained 116 exported run records. Removing 14 exact repeated records left 102 unique runs: 49 classified for this public report as flick-dry, 47 as direct-dry, and 6 invalid measurement. The 96 valid reviewed runs enter comparisons; invalid runs are excluded.

Method

Compare conditions within archive/measurement-date sets, a provisional proxy rather than verified person/dryer identity. Do not pool individual runtimes across sets. Exact duplicate start/stop/runtime signatures were counted once and their canonical labels agreed. Legacy review labels were normalized; comments were not parsed as reviews. Public condition names are flick-dry and direct-dry; the private source labels and measurements are unchanged.

Within-set median reduction (%) = 100 × (direct-dry median − flick-dry median) / direct-dry median. Medians were checked using integer-millisecond input. Displayed seconds use half-up rounding; differences are computed before rounding displayed medians.

Energy savings calculated from dryer running time

For a dryer operating at constant power, the percentage reduction in running time is also the percentage reduction in operating energy. The calculations below use several example power ratings to show how the observed runtime difference translates into energy saved.

Across the 15 comparison sets, the equal-weight average reduction in dryer running time is 5.646 seconds per use. The calculations below translate this time saving into energy savings at different example dryer power ratings.

Illustrative energy difference (Wh/use) = assumed power (W) × mean runtime difference (s) / 3600. At an assumed 1,500 W, this yields 2.353 Wh/use. The calculation uses 1,500 W as an example dryer power rating.

  • 500 W assumed: 0.784 Wh/use.
  • 1,000 W assumed: 1.568 Wh/use.
  • 1,500 W assumed: 2.353 Wh/use.
  • 2,000 W assumed: 3.137 Wh/use.
  • 2,300 W assumed: 3.607 Wh/use.

Small-sample sensitivity: Restricting to the eight sets with at least three runs per condition gives a mean difference of 4.967 seconds. This filter does not resolve the missing controls or establish independence.
